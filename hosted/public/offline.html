Nine Thousand Years of Memory: The Neolithic Village of Choirokoitia

Choirokoitia · English · free preview

Long before Cyprus had cities, palaces, or written history, people were already building round stone houses on a hillside above the Maroni river. Choirokoitia was settled almost nine thousand years ago, during the Neolithic period, making it one of the best-preserved prehistoric sites anywhere in the Eastern Mediterranean.

The people who lived here built circular dwellings from stone and mudbrick, thatched with branches and clay. They kept sheep and goats, ground grain by hand, and buried their dead beneath the floors of their own homes, a quiet way of keeping family close even in death.

In 1998, Choirokoitia was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognised as one of the most important sources of knowledge about how human communities first organised themselves into permanent settlements in this part of the world.

This pin marks a place where the story of Cyprus does not begin with any single community, but with the very first people who chose this island as home. Every culture that came after, every wave of migration this map records, has its roots in that first decision to stay.

Threads of Lefkara: A Village Stitched in Lace

Lefkara · English · free preview

In the hill village of Pano Lefkara, the sound of a Cypriot afternoon has, for centuries, included the quiet click of needles. Lefkaritika, or Lefkara lace, is said to date back to at least the fourteenth century, shaped by local needlework traditions and by the embroidery of Venetian nobility who once ruled the island.

The craft combines four techniques: hemstitching, cutwork, satin-stitch fillings and needlepoint edging, worked entirely by hand into tablecloths, veils and fine linens. It was never a solitary craft. Women gathered in the narrow streets and on shaded patios to work together, turning the making of lace into an ongoing, shared conversation passed from mother to daughter.

In 2009, UNESCO inscribed Lefkara lace on its Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage, recognising not just the craft itself but the social life woven around it.

This pin marks a village where a needle and thread became a way for generations of women to keep talking to each other, and where that conversation, stitch by stitch, is still being had today.

From Milk to Memory: The Story of Halloumi

Cyprus · English · free preview

In the villages of the Troodos foothills, cheese-making was never just a way to preserve milk through the seasons. It was a rhythm of rural life, passed down through families who kept sheep and goats on the same hillsides for generations. Their cheese, folded and salted in a way that lets it be grilled or fried without melting, became known across the island simply as halloumi.

In 2021, the European Union registered halloumi as a Protected Designation of Origin, meaning only cheese made in Cyprus to the traditional recipe may carry the name. Producers on both sides of the island were included in the protection, a rare piece of shared heritage recognised across every community.

For families in these hill villages, the cheese was never about branding. It was about a specific pot, a specific hillside, and the particular taste of milk from animals that grazed on wild thyme and oregano.

This pin marks the everyday side of cultural memory: the kind that is not found in a museum, but on a kitchen table, still being made the same way it always was.
